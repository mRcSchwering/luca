# CO2 fixation pathways

- Calvin cycle
- 3-hydroxypropionate cycle
- Wood-Ljungdahl cycle
- reductive TCA cycle
- dicarboxylate/4-hydroxybutyrate cycle
- 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate cycle

from Gong, Fuyu & Cai, Zhen & Li, Yin. (2016). Synthetic biology for CO2 fixation,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_carbon_fixation#Overview%20of%20pathways, and https://equilibrator.weizmann.ac.il
Some reactions are combined for simplicity. Common intermediates between different pathways are kept to allow cell to use combinations of pathways.
Molecule energies were solved using z3 by minimizing the absolute difference between expected reaction energies and calculated reaction energies
while only allowing a maximum per reaction error of 15 kJ/mol.
Diffusion coefficients were derived from charge and size using CO2, CO, Glucose, Sucrose, NADH, CoA as anchor points.
Permeability was deemed neglectable for all but CO2, CO, Q, QH2.
Half-lifes were assigned to the molecule's most liable group, neglecting everything above 100 days.

### Substrates

The only carbon source is CO2.
Chemical energy comes from ATP.
Reducing power comes from NADPH, QH2, Fe-red, FADH2, Fd-red.
Together these molecules are metabolic pathway substrates.
They must be provided in high concentrations to always push pathways towards fixing carbon.

### Products

Molecules that fixed carbon and can siphon out of the pathways are G3P, pyruvate, acetyl-CoA.
There must be a process that removes them from cells.
This keeps intracellular concentrations low and pulls pathways towards fixing carbon.
In case of acetyl-CoA, HS-CoA must be regenerated as it also acts as co-factor and only the acetyl group represents fixed carbon.

### Co-factors

Molecules that are re-generated by the pathways and act as seeds or co-factors are RuBP, oxalacetate, FH4, HS-CoA.
Cells cannot generate them from substrates so they must be provided initially.
When cells divide, descendants are left with halved intracellular concentrations.
Thus, co-factors need to be regularly re-supplied to allow indefinite growth.

### Carbon counting

To count accumulated carbon in cells a dummy molecule species X is introduced.
Reactions allow it to be converted from pathway products while accounting for fixed carbon atoms.
These reactions must be sufficiently exergonic to keep removing products and not stall pathways.
Cell divisions remove a fixed amount of X to prevent it from accumulating.
X should be impermeable to prevent cells from loosing it.
